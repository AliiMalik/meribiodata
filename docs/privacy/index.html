MeriBiodata — Privacy Policy

Effective 7 August 2026 · Version 1.1

We run no server, and we never see your biodata. Everything you write stays on your phone. If you switch on Google Drive backup, a copy also goes to your own Google Drive — locked with a password only you know, which means even Google cannot read it.

1. Who we are

MeriBiodata is made by Safarnama Studios. If you have a question about this policy or about your information, write to alihmalik49@gmail.com.

2. What we collect: nothing

The app deliberately asks you for some of the most personal information you have. Names, dates of birth, caste and biradari, maslak, education, income, home address, phone numbers, family details and photographs.

None of it reaches us. It is written to an encrypted database inside the app's own private storage on your phone and read back only by the app. We operate no server, so there is nowhere for your biodata to be sent to us even in principle. The app's internet permission is used by exactly three things, all described below, and our build system fails if a fourth is ever added.

3. The three things that use the internet

Google AdMob

The app shows ads on the home screen and in the form editor, supplied by Google AdMob. To do that, AdMob contacts Google over the internet and — if you allow it — may use your device's advertising ID and general device information to choose and measure ads.

  • Before the ads system starts at all, you are asked for consent through Google's own consent form.
  • If you say no, ads are switched off and the app keeps working exactly as before. Nothing is locked behind them.
  • Your biodata content is never sent to AdMob. It has no access to it.
  • What Google does with advertising data is described in the Google Privacy Policy.
  • You can reset or delete your advertising ID at any time in your phone's Settings → Privacy → Ads.

The Matchmaker Pro waitlist

If you tap the waitlist screen, the app opens a form in your own browser. Nothing is sent unless you choose to fill that form in and submit it, and the app never fills it in for you from anything you have stored. If you do submit it, the name, WhatsApp number and city you type there go to the company that hosts the form, not into the app.

The form provider and its own privacy policy will be named here once the form is live.

Google Drive backup

This is off until you switch it on. If you do, the app signs you in to your Google account and keeps one backup file in your own Google Drive, so that a lost or replaced phone does not mean a lost biodata.

  • The file is locked with a password you choose. Everything inside is encrypted on your phone before it is uploaded. Google stores the file but cannot read what is in it, and neither can we.
  • If you forget that password, the backup cannot be opened. Not by us, not by Google, not by anyone. There is no recovery.
  • The app asks for the narrowest Google Drive permission that exists (drive.file). It can only see the one file it created. It cannot read your documents, your photos, or anything else in your Drive — not by choice, but because Google does not give it access.
  • The file is visible in your Drive, named MeriBiodata-backup.mbd. It is yours: you can move it, copy it or delete it.
  • If you disconnect, this phone stops backing up. The file already in your Drive is not deleted — it is yours to remove if you want it gone.
  • What Google does with files you store in Drive is covered by the Google Privacy Policy.

4. When you share a biodata

When you export a biodata and send it — to WhatsApp, to a print shop, to anyone — that file leaves the app and is then handled by whatever you sent it through. We cannot see it, and we cannot call it back.

This is why the app offers two versions of every biodata. Wide sharing leaves out private details such as your phone number and exact address, and is the version the app always starts with. One family includes everything, and you have to choose it each time. Please choose deliberately: a biodata that goes into a WhatsApp group cannot be taken back.

5. Photographs

  • A photo you add is stored in the app's private storage, in a place other apps on your phone cannot reach.
  • Information about where and when the photo was taken is removed before it is saved. This matters more than it sounds: a photo taken at home normally carries the exact location of that home, invisibly, inside the file. The app rebuilds the picture from scratch so none of that can travel with it.
  • A photo is included in an export only when you switch it on for that export, and the switch resets whenever you change who the biodata is for.
  • Photos are never uploaded anywhere.

6. Backups

A backup is locked with a password only you know, and the locking happens on your phone before anything is uploaded. Without that password the file cannot be opened — not by us, not by Google, not by anyone — and there is no way to recover it.

Photos are inside the backup too, and are encrypted with everything else.

8. Children

MeriBiodata is meant for arranging marriages and is not directed at children. It is intended for people aged 18 and over. A biodata is often prepared by a parent or an older relative, which is expected and supported — but the app should be operated by an adult.

9. Keeping and deleting your information

There is nothing of yours on any server of ours to keep or to delete, because there is no server. Everything lives on your phone.

Uninstalling MeriBiodata removes all of it — every biodata, every photo, every setting. That is the complete answer for what is on the phone, which is why there is no separate "delete my data" button inside the app.

If you switched on Drive backup, one more thing exists: the backup file in your own Google Drive. Uninstalling the app does not remove it, because it is your file in your account and deleting it is not ours to do. Delete it from Drive whenever you want it gone.

10. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes in a way that matters, the new version will be published here with a new effective date, and the change will be pointed out inside the app.
